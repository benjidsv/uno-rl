# Step 9.5g results: where the world budget goes (2026-09-18)

> On top of commit `b00c2f7` (uncommitted). Rerun:
> `uv run uno pimc concentration 400 128 2 8 random 16x8 10` and the same with `heuristic`.
> Logs: `logs/step9.5g/`. Tests: `tests/eval/test_concentration.py`, 8 tests in 0.70 s.
> **0 failed checks** on both columns.
>
> A diagnostic step, not a ladder rung: nothing here changes an agent. It exists because a
> proposal was made -- that PIMC's uniform world sampling wastes most of its budget on worlds
> that carry the same information, and should be replaced by something closer to how a person
> reasons ("what if he has a +2? but there is only one left and he already had three") -- and the
> project's rule is to measure a proposal before building it. The proposal is **half right, and
> the half that is right is worth about a point**.

## The question, made testable

The complaint has two halves, and they need different instruments:

1. **weight worlds by likelihood.** Already built: that is the belief, and 9.5f measured it at
   **+2.83 / +4.40** points. It is also where the sharpest lesson of that step came from --
   *condition, never weight*.
2. **do not distinguish worlds that make no difference.** Not built, not measured, and the
   subject of this step. The formal version of it is a **stratified (Rao-Blackwellised)** sampler:
   partition the belief by a statistic of the hidden hand, take the stratum probabilities exactly
   from the belief, and sample within strata. Its gain is exactly the share of the per-world
   variance that the statistic explains.

So the measurement is a variance decomposition of the quantity PIMC averages. At each of the
agent's own decisions the full `(worlds, legal)` value matrix is recorded -- the matrix it really
averaged, not a re-probe -- and everything below is post-processing of it. `_Probe` is checked to
replay a plain `PimcSearch` action for action before anything is read off it.

`FULL_UNO`, w = 128, d = 2, `response` continuation, `voids` belief, 400 DEV deals, both
seatings, against both columns. ~13,000 decisions with a choice per column.

## 1. The worlds do **not** agree (the proposal I made first is wrong)

The hypothesis that motivated the run was that most decisions are unanimous across worlds, so the
budget is spent confirming an answer already reached. It is not:

| column | decisions | mean legal | mean distinct argmaxes | unanimous | winner's share of worlds |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Random | 13,957 | 4.49 | **3.59** | **6.6 %** | 65.4 % |
| Heuristic | 12,500 | 4.27 | **3.56** | **5.8 %** | 64.4 % |

Nearly four of the 4.5 legal actions win at least one world. Only one decision in fifteen is
unanimous, and at `color` nodes none are. **PIMC's worlds disagree about the best action almost
everywhere**, which kills "the budget confirms a foregone conclusion" outright.

## 2. The decisions are far noisier than the score ladder suggests

| prefix of k worlds names the w = 128 action | k=1 | k=4 | k=16 | **k=32** | k=64 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| vs Random | 61.8 % | 71.9 % | 83.0 % | **87.8 %** | 92.6 % |
| vs Heuristic | 59.6 % | 70.2 % | 82.1 % | **87.2 %** | 92.0 % |

Two **disjoint** 64-world halves of the same decision agree only **85.6 % / 84.0 %** of the time.

This is a new fact about the instrument, and it bears on 9.5f section 7.2. That step found the
score ladder flat past w = 32 (`voids` 32 -> 64 is +1.20 / +0.77, z 1.1 / 0.7) and made w = 32 the
working set. Flat in **score** turns out to coexist with **12 % churn in the decisions**: the
shipped agent disagrees with a four-times-larger sample at one decision in eight, and scores the
same. The reconciliation is in the last column of the next table -- what the churn is worth per
changed decision is small, so the disagreement is overwhelmingly between near-equal actions. The
working set is not undermined; but "the ladder is flat" must not be read as "the agent has
converged", and a future attempt to *measure* something through PIMC's action choices (an
attribution, a distillation target) has to know it is reading a 12 %-noisy signal.

## 3. Adaptive stopping: the one lever this run found

Stopping when the leader's t-statistic over the runner-up clears `t`, checked every 4 worlds.
Post-processing of the recorded worlds in the order they were drawn, so it is exact for this rule.

| t | mean worlds | saved | action differs from w = 128 | value given up per changed decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 13.1 | 89.8 % | 14.7 % | 0.032 |
| 2.0 | 41.0 | 68.0 % | 4.4 % | 0.032 |
| **3.0** | **61.1** | **52.3 %** | **1.9 %** | 0.035 |
| 4.0 | 74.7 | 41.6 % | 1.3 % | 0.031 |

(vs Random; the Heuristic column is the same shape, 63.4 worlds and 3.1 % at t = 3.)

Read against section 2: **flat w = 32 reproduces the w = 128 answer 87.8 % of the time; racing to
a mean of 61 worlds reproduces it 98.1 % of the time.** So the rule buys w = 128 decision quality
for about 1.9x the w = 32 cost instead of 4x. It is not the "nearly free" saving the unanimity
hypothesis predicted -- half the budget, not nine tenths -- but it is the only change this run
found that is worth more than it costs, and it is a dozen lines on top of `_scores`.

Caveat, priced rather than hidden: the leader is chosen from the same prefix that tests it
(winner's curse), so the rule stops optimistically. The `action differs` column measures the
consequence directly.

## 4. The ceiling on the proposal: the hand carries half the spread, the pile the other half

A world is a hand **and** a pile order. Only the hand is an opponent hypothesis an abstraction
could bucket; the pile is chance the agent must average over regardless. 16 hands from the belief
x 8 orders of the remainder, at every 10th decision, `D` = (leader - runner-up) per world:

| allocation | R² | permutation control | **net** | worlds worth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| proportional | 53.1 % | 11.7 % | **46.8 %** | **1.88x** |
| Neyman (optimal) | 62.8 % | 20.5 % | **53.2 %** | **2.14x** |

Identical on the Heuristic column (47.0 % / 53.3 %, 1.89x / 2.14x).

So **the proposal's premise is confirmed and bounded in one number.** Worlds are not
interchangeable -- knowing the exact hand removes about half the spread, and the intuition that
the hidden hand is what the sampler should be organised around is right. But the other ~47 % is
pile order, which no hand abstraction touches, and *perfect* knowledge of the hand's value is
worth **1.9x** the effective world count (2.1x if allocation is optimised as well as stratified).

The Neyman row is the fair version of the "+2" intuition specifically: optimal allocation
over-samples a rare stratum with a large effect, which is exactly what proportional allocation
would miss. It adds 6 points over proportional -- real, and small.

**The arithmetic that closes it.** 9.5f measured what doubling the world count is worth:
`voids` 32 -> 64 is **+1.20 / +0.77, z 1.1 / 0.7** -- about a point, and not established. The
ceiling on stratification is 1.88-2.14x effective worlds, i.e. *that same step*. **A perfect
hand abstraction is worth roughly one score point, at the significance 9.5f could not reach.**

## 5. And the statistics a human would name recover a quarter of that ceiling

Measured on section 4's own worlds, so this is two groupings of one sample -- the exact hand
against a coarsening of it -- and `of the ceiling` is a like-for-like ratio.

| statistic of the opponent's hand | net R² (Random) | net R² (Heuristic) | net Neyman | of the ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cards playable now | 12.5 % | 12.5 % | 16.6-17.3 % | 27 % |
| cards in the current colour | 12.1 % | 12.3 % | 16.4-17.0 % | 26 % |
| action cards held | 5.2 % | 5.4 % | 8.5-8.6 % | 11-12 % |
| **holds a +4** | **2.1 %** | **2.5 %** | 2.9-3.3 % | **4-5 %** |
| **holds a +2** | **2.5 %** | **3.3 %** | 4.2-5.0 % | **5-7 %** |
| the three jointly | 18.3 % | 19.8 % | 24.9-25.5 % | 39-42 % |

Two readings, and the second is the sharper one:

1. **The best realistic bucketing is worth 1.22-1.25x worlds**, i.e. under half of the ~1 point
   the perfect one is worth. That is below what 9.5f could resolve in 3,000 EVAL deals.
2. **The example that motivated the proposal is the worst feature in the table.** "Does he have a
   +2 / a +4" -- the salient, nameable, frightening card -- explains **2-3 %** of the spread,
   4-7 % of the ceiling. What signal there is sits in dull *count* statistics: how many of his
   cards play at all, how many are in the colour. The thing that makes a world matter to this
   agent at depth 2 is not which scary card the opponent holds but how many outs he has.

## 6. Verdict

- **The mechanism is real**: worlds differ in ways that move the answer (section 1), and the
  hidden hand accounts for about half of it (section 4).
- **The remedy does not pay**: stratifying on any hand statistic is capped at ~1 point, which is
  9.5f's already-unestablished 32 -> 64 step, and a realistic statistic delivers under half of
  that. Building a stratified sampler would be building a lever below this project's own
  resolution -- and 9.5f spent `EVAL_SEEDS`, so establishing it would cost a fresh seed block
  for an effect smaller than the one that block could not settle last time.
- **The stated intuition picks the wrong feature.** Rare high-impact cards explain almost
  nothing; playable-count does the work.
- **The incidental finding is the useful one**: PIMC's action choice is ~12 % noisy at the working
  set while its score is flat, and an adaptive stopping rule reaches w = 128 agreement at ~1.9x
  the w = 32 cost.

## 7. Checks and caveats

- `_Probe` is verified to replay a plain `PimcSearch` action for action on 8 games before any
  number is read off it. It restates `_worlds_q`'s body and `determinize_l1` consumes a *variable*
  number of rng draws under `voids`, so a reordering would redraw the worlds silently.
- Every R² here is a **plug-in** ratio and upward biased with few draws per stratum, so each is
  reported against a **permutation control** on the same grouping and the `net` column is
  `(R² - control) / (1 - control)`. `tests/eval/test_concentration.py` pins that the control sits
  at the bias `(k-1)/(n-1)` rather than at zero -- a test asserting "noise scores ~0" would have
  passed a broken implementation and hidden the correction's reason for existing.
- The two columns were run independently and agree to a decimal on section 4 (46.8 / 47.0 %,
  53.2 / 53.3 %). That is the strongest evidence the decomposition is a property of the game and
  the estimator rather than of the opponent.
- **Do not read the wall-clock times** in the logs (253 s against 84 s for equal work): the
  machine was contended.
- **The trajectory is the w = 128 agent's own**, not the shipped w = 32 row's, so the state
  distribution is that agent's. Second-order for these shares, but not nothing.
- **The decomposition describes this estimator**, not the game: d = 2 with 9.5c's leaf network,
  which reads pile *length* and not order, so pile order enters only through the two searched
  plies. A deeper search would if anything give the pile *more* of the spread, which strengthens
  the conclusion rather than weakening it.
- DEV seeds throughout: a diagnostic, and `EVAL_SEEDS` is spent (09.5f-results.md section 7.3).
