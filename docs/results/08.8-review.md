## A. Methodology

### A1. The exploitability of BR L1(random) depends on sets Random never reaches

`uno.compiled.solvers.best_response` chooses, at every responder information set,
the action orbit with the best reach-weighted value. At a set with zero
counterfactual reach (chance x opponent) every orbit ties, and the solver takes
the first. That choice does not change the best response's value against its
opponent, but it is what the best response plays if someone else steers the game
there. So the exploitability of "BR L1(random)" as a row agent is partly a
property of an arbitrary fill-in. Random (PlayFirstRandom) never draws with a
play in hand, so on the free-play toys an exploiter reaches many such sets simply
by drawing.

Measured on the cached quotient trees:

| ruleset | L1 sets never reached by Random (seat 0 / seat 1) | exploitability as solved (pts) | with those sets filled by Nash |
|---------|---------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| `TOY` | 42 % / 47 % | 1.56 / 0.36 | 1.38 / 0.29 |
| `TOY_FULL` | 76 % / 84 % | 4.55 / 1.26 | 3.06 / 2.44 |
| `TOY2` | 52 % / 60 % | 1.68 / 2.17 | 1.41 / 1.70 |
| `TOY2_FULL` | 85 % / 91 % | 6.25 / 4.89 | 3.40 / 3.30 |

The value of the best response against Random is identical under both fill-ins
(0.7934, 0.8017, 0.7305, ...). "Playing well against Random is no longer
near-Nash" ([step 8.6](08.6-symmetry.md), [step 8.7](08.7-toy2.md) sections 4
and 6) survives at about half the size. The per-seat numbers and the
attribution to the voluntary draw are not supported as written.

Fix: Phase 3, M2 (an explicit fill-in).

### A2. The on-seeds check is not calibrated

The simulation check compares `|sim - on_seeds|` with the half-width of the
simulated score's CI. That CI uses the total variance of a game's score (deal,
pile and actions). But `on_seeds` already conditions on the deal and pile of
each seed, so under the null the difference only has the action-noise variance:
the mean over seeds of Var(score | seed). The check is therefore conservative
and weak at catching bugs. For the same reason, the "2.1 sigma" miss of the
`TOY2` BR L2 seat 0 cell ([step 8.7](08.7-toy2.md) section 7) is more significant
than it reads. On the three seed ranges tried for that cell, the simulation came
out below the exact value each time (-1.35, -0.04, -0.58 points).

Fix: Phase 3, M1 (a z-score on the exact conditional variance).

### A3. Canonicalising an observation does not make an agent symmetric

The step 8.6 plan for RL agents was "canonical observation in, action mapped
back". At a canonical key whose stabilizer is not trivial, several group elements
reach the canonical key, `canonical` returns one of them, and the action played
depends on which. The agent's output must also be averaged within the stabilizer's
action orbits; otherwise `tabulate` rejects it, or, played through the engine, it
is not the policy that was evaluated. The spec also said symmetrising a policy
means "averaging its images" and cited convexity. That holds for *mixing* the
images (a mixed strategy), not for averaging behaviour probabilities set by set.

Fix: [spec section 18](../spec.md) corrected; Phase 3, M3 (a `Symmetrized` wrapper).

### A4. The mirrored moments are not tested against their exact values

`uno validate` checks the per-seat means but not the correlation, split share or
variance ratio against the exact moments it prints. With the normal-theory
standard error of a correlation, three of the ten rows in [step 8.7](08.7-toy2.md)
section 7 sit about 2.1-2.3 standard errors from exact: `TOY2` heuristic vs
random, `TOY2` BR L1(random) vs heuristic, `TOY2_SKIP` heuristic vs random. That
formula may be off for scores in {0, 1/2, 1}, so this is not evidence of a bug.
But the correlation is the statistic that caught the rng coupling of step 8.6.

Fix: Phase 3, M4 (bootstrap CIs).

### A5. Checked and fine

- **The voluntary draw's value is not the claim.** On the free-play toys a player
  can draw on an empty pile to end the game (a claim, toy-only). Forbidding the
  claim with a play in hand to the responder leaves BR L1 unchanged to 4
  decimals, against Random and the Heuristic, both seats, on `TOY_FULL` and
  `TOY2_FULL`. Best responders never claim; the Nash agent claims 0.04-0.09 times
  per game ([index](index.md)).
- **The keying attach of `uno toy2_columns` is safe.** All 12 node arrays of the
  `TOY2_FULL` L2 tree equal those of the L1 tree, and BR L2 against Random
  recomputes to 0.8443 / 0.7508. The identical 0.8443 on `TOY_FULL` is a
  coincidence.
- The orbit best response, the orbit averaging in CFR, DCFR's (1.5, 0, 2), the
  PCFR+ update and the exact-mirrored independence after the step 8.6 rng fix
  look right. So does the other arithmetic of the seat-gap, exploitability and
  information-gap tables, apart from B1.

## Methodology fixes

### M1. A calibrated on-seeds check (A2)

`uno.reference.solve.scores_on_seeds(rs, agents, seeds, levels)` returns, per
seat, the exact score mean over the games of the seeds and the mean over the
seeds of Var(score | seed), computed from each seed's outcome distribution.
A simulation on those seeds is checked with z = (sim - mean) / sqrt(cond_var / n)
and |z| <= 3, i.e. 0.27 % false alarms per check. Two deterministic agents have
zero conditional variance and must match to the digit. `evaluate_on_seeds`
keeps its return values and now comes from the same function. The check is used in
`ladder`, `mirror`, `cfr`, `validate` and the tests (`tests/reference/test_solve.py`,
`tests/eval/test_estimate.py`, `tests/compiled/test_cfr.py`).

### M2. An explicit fill-in for unreached sets (A1)

`uno.compiled.solvers.best_response(..., completion=None)` counts the responder sets
with zero counterfactual reach (`BestResponse.unreached`). When a completion
strategy over the same keying is given, it plays that strategy there instead of the
first orbit; the value against the opponent is unchanged by construction.
`uno.compiled.strategies.br_row(tree, opponent, completion)` assembles both seats
into one row. The exploitability tables of `symmetry`, `toy2`, `cfr` and `validate` print BR
L1(random) both as solved and as `+nash`, with its unreached sets filled with
the Nash strategy, and the Nash row / column for both. Tested on small
rulesets: the unreached sets are exactly the sets the reference walker does not
reach under the opponent's support, the value does not move, and the row
replays to each seat's value (`tests/compiled/test_compiled.py`).

### M3. A symmetric wrapper for any agent (A3)

`uno.agents.symmetrized.Symmetrized(agent, rs)` canonicalises the
observation, asks the agent on the canonical observation and legal actions,
averages its output within the action orbits of the canonical key's stabilizer,
and maps it back. Tested: it commutes with the group on random games over
random rulesets, it leaves the Heuristic unchanged, and a wrapped asymmetric
agent (lowest type id) tabulates on the quotient with the value it has on the
full tree and through the engine (`tests/game/test_symmetry.py`).

### M4. Calibrated moment checks (A4)

`uno.eval.estimate.bootstrap_moments(pairs)` gives seeded percentile bootstrap CIs
over the pairs for the per-seat variances, the correlation, the split share and
the variance ratio; `covers(exact)` names the exact moments that fall outside.
The level is 99.8 % per moment, a family-wise 99 % for the five moments of a
matchup (Bonferroni), with 4000 resamples. It is used in `validate`, `mirror`,
`cfr` and `tests/eval/test_estimate.py` (instead of the fixed 10 % / 0.06 / 0.03
tolerances).

The first version used 99 % per moment, and the stochastic-row test failed on
it: the exact split share of Heuristic vs Random on `TOY` (0.5956) fell outside
the CI on the dev seeds (sampled 0.6120, z = +2.1). The same comparison on five
disjoint seed ranges of 4000 pairs:

| seeds | split share | z | corr | all five moments covered (99 %) |
|-------|-------------|---|------|---------------------------------|
| 0-3999 | 0.6120 | +2.11 | -0.331 | yes, with 1000 resamples |
| 4000-7999 | 0.5910 | -0.60 | -0.298 | yes |
| 8000-11999 | 0.6050 | +1.21 | -0.313 | yes |
| 1,000,000-1,003,999 | 0.5915 | -0.53 | -0.277 | yes |
| 2,000,000-2,003,999 | 0.5823 | -1.72 | -0.274 | yes |
| pooled, 20,000 pairs | 0.5964 | +0.21 | -0.299 (Fisher z +0.18) | |

Exact: split share 0.5956, correlation -0.2997. There is no residual coupling of
the two games of a pair. The dev seeds drew high, and five moments at 99 % each
make such a miss about a one-in-twenty event per matchup, hence the Bonferroni
level. The same scatter accounts for the ~2 SE correlations of A4.

### The one miss: `TOY2`, BR L2 seat 0 against Random

It is the same cell that missed at "2.1 sigma" in step 8.7, and now misses at
|z| > 3 under the calibrated check, so it was examined before moving on. The
same best responses (L1, L2, L3, seat 0) simulated on six disjoint ranges of
4000 seeds, z against the exact action noise of each range:

| level | 0 | 4000 | 8000 | 12000 | 1,000,000 (held out) | 2,000,000 | pooled, 24,000 seeds |
|-------|---|------|------|-------|----------------------|-----------|----------------------|
| L1 | -0.10 | -1.00 | +1.00 | +2.35 | -0.70 | -1.35 | +0.07 |
| L2 | +0.77 | -1.21 | +0.15 | +1.54 | -3.01 | -1.83 | -1.48 |
| L3 | +0.69 | -0.51 | +0.47 | +1.63 | -2.21 | -1.05 | -0.42 |

No level deviates when pooled (|z| < 1.5), and the three share the held-out
range's low draw: they face the same Random action streams on the same seeds, so
their deviations are correlated. The miss is chance: about 100 on-seeds checks
across the four runs make one |z| just above 3 roughly a one-in-four event. The
threshold stays at 3 per check, chosen before the re-run.

4. Nash-filled is one reasonable fill-in, not the least exploitable one; solving the
   unreached sets for minimal exploitability would give lower numbers still, and
   was not done.
