## What was built

- `uno/eval/search.py`: `determinize_l1(..., belief=...)` and `PimcSearch(belief=...)`. Without a
  belief the behaviour is byte-identical to 9.5e's, which the 22 pre-existing tests still pin.

- Tests: `tests/eval/test_belief.py`, 9 tests in 0.70 s.

## 1. The design in the plan does not work

`plan.md` specified a **particle filter over the deal**: sample the opponent's initial hand,
replay the public history, weight each particle by the opponent's action probabilities, resample
per step. Built first, and measured before any score was believed:

| opponent's play | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| share of 64 particles still alive | 0.156 | 0.018 | 0.002 | 0.0004 | 0 |

A particle survives an observed play only if it happens to hold the played card, which is
`hand_size / unseen` ~ 7/98 on full UNO. The decay is geometric, so **no particle count fixes it**:
by the fourth play one would need ~10^5 particles, and it keeps decaying. The implementation that
reset on collapse (62 % of decisions) scored **worse than the prior it replaced** -- Brier 0.167
against the uniform split's 0.085, with its "probability 1.000" bin observed at 0.112.

This was caught by a calibration harness written *before* the score was measured, not by the score
itself. Sharpness alone would have looked like progress; calibration is what says a belief is
*correct* rather than merely confident. The lesson generalises: **condition, never weight.** A
world must be built consistent with what was observed, not sampled and then tested against it.

2. **The soft likelihood buys nothing measurable over the hard constraints.** `window - voids` is

   the rung that reads **no policy probabilities**. *(Written here as "no opponent policy at all";
   section 8 corrects that -- it does carry a behavioural assumption.)*
3. **So the cheap half is the half worth keeping.** `voids` costs +10 % (139.6 against 126.5
   ms/game) where `window` costs +29 %, and it needs none of the opponent's action probabilities.
   `voids` is the recommended default. *(The stronger claim made here -- that it therefore survives
   into a general player -- is withdrawn in section 8.)*
4. **The pre-declared prediction is confirmed.** The toys measure `posterior - prior` at 0.0-0.6
   points against Random (up to 2.6 against the Heuristic), which is why 9.5e deferred this step.
   Full UNO gives **+2.83 / +4.40**, five to ten times the toy figure against the same column. The
   mechanism predicted when the step was scoped is what `voids` exploits: a full-UNO game runs 41

   is a hard statement, where a 6-decision toy has almost none. This is 9.5d's "toy belief sizes
   do not transfer" pointing the other way.
5. **Do not read the per-seat ordering.** On DEV the gain was larger at seat 0 (+4.20 / +1.80); on
   EVAL it is larger at seat 1 for `voids`. Individual seats move by more than their difference;
   only the both-seats claim is supported.

## 3. Why the constraint is worth so much

Measured on full UNO before the rung was written: the void constraint alone rules out **79.9 %** of
`determinize_l1`'s worlds (median surviving share 15.6 %, so **w = 64 is effectively w = 10**), and
the opponent's real hand violated it **0 times in 2,487** decisions. At least one constraint is
live at **73.4 %** of full-UNO decisions.

That also bears on 9.5e's open question. That step found the world ladder still flat past 32 with
the continuation and the seat networks both fixed, and promoted the off-profile leaf network to
"the residual explanation". A second explanation was available and is now measured: **most of the
worlds were impossible**, so the extra ones were not adding information. Whether the ladder
un-flattens once the belief is correct is the natural next test and is **not** settled here.

## 4. Checks

- **Calibration, not sharpness**, is the correctness test, and it is what refuted section 1. On
  120 DEV deals over 120,712 (card type, decision) pairs, Brier: prior 0.0846, `voids` 0.0828,
  `window` 0.0825, with predicted tracking observed in every populated bin (0.041 -> 0.050,
  0.142 -> 0.135, 0.234 -> 0.208, 0.428 -> 0.396). A belief that over-prunes is sharper *and*
  mis-calibrated; this one is neither.
- **The marginal sharpening is small (2 %) while 79.9 % of worlds are excluded**, which is not a
  contradiction: the constraint rules out joint combinations while barely moving any single card's
  marginal. Brier on marginals is the right *correctness* check and the wrong *value* check, which
  is why section 2 is scored and not inferred from it.
- `tests/eval/test_belief.py` pins the two truth-survives properties (`voids` never excludes the
  hand the opponent really holds, on `FULL_UNO` and `TOY_FULL`; the `window` likelihood is
  strictly positive for it), a non-vacuity guard, world consistency, and that the agent is still a
  deterministic function of its information state with a belief wired in.
- **The `belief` run's "0 failed checks" line means nothing.** That section registers no `check()`
  assertions of its own, unlike `uno` and `d2`, so its counter is trivially zero. Validation for
  this step is the tests plus `uv run uno pimc calibration`, which *does* carry real checks (no
  rung materially worse than the exact prior; no populated reliability bin missing by more than
  0.25, where the refuted filter missed by 0.89) and reports the fallback rates that `belief`
  cannot, since it runs single-process. `belief` itself still needs checks.

## 6. What is left on the table

1. **`window` is untested where it should matter.** The column here is Random, which is nearly
   uniform, so its play choices carry little information -- and the toys agree, putting
   `posterior - prior` at 0.0-0.6 against Random but up to **2.6 against the Heuristic**. The
   vs-Heuristic column is where the soft rung should earn its 29 %, and it has not been run.
   *Partly addressed in section 7: the column is now a parameter and was run, but only `prior`
   and `voids` completed before the run was stopped, so `window` against the Heuristic -- the
   actual go/no-go -- is still unmeasured.*
2. **No toy closure test for this axis.** Every other sampled method in 9.5b-9.5e was closed
   against an exact toy value, and this one is not: `window` is a *windowed* posterior, so it need
   not equal `uno.compiled.pimc`'s `"posterior"`, and what it should equal has not been derived.
   `TOY2` vs the Heuristic at seat 1 separates the belief axis by 2.62 points and `voids` is live
   there (23.3 % of decisions), so the cell exists; the assertion does not yet.
3. **Exploitability is unmeasured and the toys warn about it.** There, fitting the belief to one
   opponent *costs* exploitability (`TOY2_SKIP` vs the Heuristic: 3.52 / 6.43 for
   `posterior/response` against 1.78 / 2.29 for `prior/response`). `voids` should be exempt, since
   it reads no policy, but that is an argument and not a measurement.
   *Section 8 withdraws the premise: `voids` does carry a behavioural assumption, so it is a
   candidate for being exploitable rather than exempt.*
4. **Does the world ladder un-flatten?** Section 3: the belief roughly sextuples the effective
   world count, so the w = 32 -> 128 ladder should be re-run with `voids` on. If it now climbs,
   the leaf network is *not* the binding constraint 9.5e concluded it was.
   *Answered in section 7 -- and the test proposed here turned out **not to be decisive as
   designed**. The ladder does not un-flatten, but flatness is consistent with both explanations,
   so it never could have separated them. What does separate them is a comparison this item did
   not think to ask for: `voids` at w = 32 outscores `prior` at w = 128.*
5. **The `belief` section still registers no checks** (section 4) and its fallback-rate line is
   inert under multiprocessing, the counters not crossing `pmap`'s process boundary. The
   calibration harness was promoted into the repo as `uv run uno pimc calibration` with two real
   checks and working fallback rates (4.8 % / 4.9 %), but `belief` itself was left as it is.
6. **`window` is the least calibrated rung** -- worst reliability-bin gap 0.135 against `voids`'
   0.093 and the prior's 0.088 -- while being marginally the sharpest (Brier 0.0825). That is what
   a *windowed* posterior should look like, conditioning on a truncated history and so slightly
   biased, and it is a second mark against `window` beside its failure to beat `voids` on score.

## 7. Follow-ups: the belief inside the world ladder, and against a second column

> Reruns: `uv run uno pimc sweep 3000 "" "s8x2r,s8x2rv,s32x2r,s32x2rv,s64x2rv,s128x2r,s128x2rv"`
> and `uv run uno pimc belief 4000 32 2 "" heuristic`. Logs: `logs/step9.5f/`.
> Tests: **219 passed in 7 min 16 s** (206 above; the 13 new ones are `tests/eval/test_pimc_configs.py`).
> **The sweep was stopped at w = 64 by choice**, so its two w = 128 configurations are missing and
> every difference in 7.2 is **unpaired** -- the paired blocks are computed from arrays held in
> memory and print only at the end. Pairing removes 11-19 % of the standard error on that axis, so
> those errors are about 16 % wider than a completed run's, not different in kind. The belief run
> of 7.3 completed all three rungs and **is** paired.

Items 1 and 4 above each needed a harness change first: `belief_section` hardcoded Random as the
column, and `sweep` could not take a belief at all (its meta tuples are unpacked as
`("search", w, d, cont)` in several places). Both are now parameters -- the belief as its **own**
field of the meta tuple, never folded into the continuation letter, since the ladder groupings
filter on that letter and would have silently dropped every row they touched instead of failing.

### 7.1 The no-belief path is provably unchanged

The `prior` rows deliberately keep step 9.5e's bare agent names (`pimc_resp_random`), so they share
that step's world-sampling key: same name, same information state, same worlds, same games. They
reproduced 9.5e **to the digit, including the standard errors** -- `s8x2r` 0.7010 +- 0.0084 /
0.6833 +- 0.0085 and `s32x2r` 0.7567 +- 0.0078 / 0.7167 +- 0.0082. The rewiring left the path the
new numbers are measured against alone, and this is a cheaper regression test than any assertion:
a baseline that quietly moved would have shown up in the fourth decimal.

### 7.2 The world ladder under `voids` (3,000 EVAL deals, response continuation)

| w | `prior` s0 / s1 | `voids` s0 / s1 | `voids - prior` | ms/game, prior -> voids |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 0.7010 / 0.6833 | 0.7367 / 0.7173 | **+3.57 / +3.40** (z 3.1 / 2.9) | 34.9 -> 36.5 |
| 32 | 0.7567 / 0.7167 | **0.7813 / 0.7603** | **+2.46 / +4.36** (z 2.3 / 4.0) | 124.1 -> 138.6 |
| 64 | 0.7573 / 0.7343 * | **0.7933 / 0.7680** | **+3.60 / +3.37** (z 3.3 / 3.1) | 271.0 * -> 273.7 |
| 128 | 0.7677 / 0.7403 * | not run | | 530.0 * |

\* from step 9.5e, which section 7.1 shows this run replays exactly.

1. **The belief is a level shift, not a change of slope.** It is worth ~+3.5 points at *every*
   world count, and the two ladders have the same shape: steep to w = 32 (`voids` 8 -> 32 is
   +4.46 / +4.30) and flat after (`voids` 32 -> 64 is +1.20 / +0.77, z 1.1 / 0.7, against `prior`'s
   32 -> 128 of ~+1.1 at z ~ 1.0).
2. **So item 4's test was not decisive as designed.** A flat `voids` ladder is consistent with the
   leaf network capping the search *and* with `voids` simply saturating the world count early
   (w = 32 carrying ~32 possible worlds where `prior`'s carried ~6). Flatness alone cannot separate
   them, which the item assumed it could.
3. **What does separate them was not asked for.** `voids` at **w = 32** (0.7813 / 0.7603) outscores
   `prior` at **w = 128** (0.7677 / 0.7403) *and* at w = 256 (0.7660 / 0.7347), for a quarter and an
   eighth of the cost. So 9.5e's plateau was **not a ceiling imposed by the leaf value function**:
   better worlds went straight through it. The leaf network is demoted from "the residual
   explanation" back to one constraint among others, and world *quality* is established as a lever
   that moves the plateau itself rather than the climb towards it.
4. **The operating point changes.** `index.md`'s "the usable setting is 64-128 worlds at depth 2"
   is superseded: **w = 32 with `voids`** beats it outright at a quarter of the cost. For measuring
   *differences between variants* w = 32 is also the better instrument -- w = 64 is 1.97x the cost
   for an unestablished +1.20 / +0.77, and at a fixed wall clock w = 32 buys twice the deals, so a
   3-point effect reaches z ~ 3.2 where w = 64 reaches z ~ 2.2. It also sits on the *flat* part of
   the ladder, where a comparison is insensitive to the exact world count.
5. **The caveat this ladder has earned twice.** 9.5e reopened 9.5d's ladder (the continuation rule)
   and this reopens 9.5e's (the belief). Both times because something changed what a *world is*.
   Treat "w = 32" as valid until world quality changes again, and re-check the shape when it does.

### 7.4 Batched leaves: 4.7x, and the numbers are unchanged

The belief run above was executed after the leaf evaluations were batched per decision
(`batched_search_q`) and `unseen_cards` was hoisted out of the per-world loop. The `prior` and
`voids` rows reproduce the pre-batching partial run **to four decimals including the standard
errors** (0.6175 / 0.5897 and 0.6402 / 0.6268), which is the property that matters: the batched
agent replays the unbatched one's games rather than merely scoring like them. On this run's own
clock the cost went 76.2 -> 16.0 and 88.9 -> 19.7 ms/game.

The speed-up is two passes, not one, and the controlled before/after with its per-candidate
breakdown -- including the three candidates dropped *because* they were measured first, one of them
because it would have resampled the ladder rather than replayed it -- is in docs/context.md section
5.21. The part that matters here is the **asymmetry**: `prior` and `voids` gained 4.4-4.5x while
`window` gained only 2.6x, because its likelihood is Python-side and batches with nothing. That is
what repriced `window` from 1.29x `voids` to 2.4x, and it is the arithmetic behind 7.3's decision.

Worth recording because the failure mode was specific: a `voids` world costs a *variable* number of
rng draws (rejection sampling), so any reordering of the sampling loop changes which worlds are
drawn without changing how many, and the scores would have moved by a point or two with nothing
obviously broken. Byte-identity against a pre-optimisation run is what rules that out, and 7.1's
cross-run anchors do the same job for the sweep.

### 7.5 What is still open

- **The toy closure test** (item 2) is untouched and remains the one place this step falls short of
  the project's standard. Now cheaper than when it was scoped: `voids` is the rung that ships, and
  closing `voids` alone is a smaller job than closing a *windowed* posterior with no exact
  counterpart.
- **Exploitability of the rungs** (item 3) is still unmeasured, and it is the one that matters for
  steps 10-11: the toys warn that fitting a belief to one opponent *costs* exploitability
  (`TOY2_SKIP`, `posterior/response` 3.52 / 6.43 against `prior/response`'s 1.78 / 2.29). Section 8
  removes the reason `voids` was expected to be exempt, and gives the measurement a prediction to
  test: a best response should learn to **keep** playable drawn cards, the line the rung rules out.
- **`window` at seat 1** needs a seed range beyond `EVAL_SEEDS`, which is exhausted.
- **The ladder past w = 64.** Lower value than it looked: 7.2 shows the shape question was answered

- **A general (column-free) full-UNO PIMC** has never been measured. Both levers that carry the
  row's points -- the `response` continuation and `window` -- read the column's policy, and
  section 8 shows `voids` does not survive unchanged into a player that does not know who it is
  facing either, which is what step 11 needs.

## 8. The toy closure test, and what it found about `voids`

> Rerun: `uv run uno pimc closure TOY 20 4000 3`. **0 failed checks.** Suite 245 passed.
> This is item 2 of section 6 -- the one place this step fell short of the project's standard of
> closing a sampled method against an exact toy value before trusting it on full UNO. It was built
> and it failed twice before passing, which is the argument for the standard.

### 8.1 What the rung is closed against, and how well

`voids` is **not** closed against `uno.compiled.pimc`'s `"prior"`. The constraint encodes an
assumption about the opponent, so the exact object it moves towards is the `"posterior"` weighting
-- the prior times that opponent's reach -- which is what `uno.eval.belief` says the module is for
("narrows that split towards the posterior"). Scored against the prior instead, the test measures
the assumption rather than the implementation and calls a correct rung broken; it did exactly that,
at 0.23 against 0.13, before the target was fixed.

On the **full** (non-symmetric) `TOY` world tree -- member worlds of a quotient set are each
canonicalised by their own group element, so an opponent hand read off a member's L3 key would be
in the wrong coordinates -- over constrained sets with at least 3 consistent hands:

| total variation to the exact posterior | seat 0 | seat 1 |
|---|---|---|
| uniform split (`prior`, the belief PIMC held through 9.5d-9.5e) | 0.4325 | 0.4496 |
| **`voids`** | **0.0502** | **0.0407** |
| distance the rung is trying to close (exact prior -> exact posterior) | 0.2939 | 0.3254 |
| sampling noise, two independent draws of the same rung | 0.0070 | 0.0041 |

**`voids` closes 89-91 % of it.** That is the closure the step lacked. It also quantifies what the
uniform split was worth as a belief: 0.43-0.45 away from the truth, which is why the rung bought
real points rather than a rounding error.

### 8.2 The rung is a policy assumption, not a rules fact

The constraint counts cards drawn *strictly after* the void, so it assumes the card drawn **at**
the void did not stay in hand as a playable one -- that a playable drawn card is played at once.
Every play-first rulebot does. It is not a rule: `draw_then_may_play` lets the drawer keep it by
passing, and a `Pass` writes no public event (section 3 of the context doc), so nothing in the
history distinguishes "could not play" from "chose not to".

Measured three ways, all on code as shipped:

| | result |
|---|---|
| `TOY` tree, 5,001 constrained sets/seat | the constraint **empties 32.0 % (seat 0) / 50.3 % (seat 1)** of them -- every consistent world excluded, so the real hand among them |
| the obvious repair, counting the void's own draw | keeps **100 %** of worlds at both seats: it excludes nothing at all |
| `FULL_UNO`, opponent keeping a playable drawn card half the time | **144 / 1193 = 12.1 %** of constrained decisions rule out the hand they really hold (**0 / 837** against a rulebot) |

The middle row is not an accident, it is a proof: at the void the hand holds nothing playable, and
every card that could be playable since was drawn since, so "playable now <= drawn since" is a
tautology. **There is no assumption-free, non-vacuous version** -- 8.7 measures why, on the ruleset that
matters. There is, however, a strictly *less*-assuming one, and it is worth recording: counting the
cards the opponent has since **played** as well as those they hold, `held + played <= since + 1`,
needs only "never draws holding a playable card" and drops "always plays a playable drawn card".
It is not vacuous -- it prunes 43-48 % of worlds with **0** truth exclusions against both a rulebot
and an opponent that keeps drawn cards, where the shipped rung excludes the truth 12.1 % of the
time against the latter. It still fails (71 %) against an opponent that draws *while holding* a
playable card, since that breaks the premise itself rather than the accounting. The rung is inherently policy-dependent.

What survives is a weaker, still useful claim: `voids` needs no policy **probabilities**, which is
what separates it from `window` and keeps it cheap and column-portable. What is withdrawn is
"reads no opponent policy" and everything resting on it -- in particular that it is the half that
survives unchanged into a general player.

### 8.3 Why that is consistent with the scores

Against the opponent it assumes, the rung is exact:

| seat | constrained sets | `voids` empties | posterior already zero | **posterior non-zero** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 5,001 | 1,599 | 1,599 | **0** |
| 1 | 5,001 | 2,518 | 2,518 | **0** |

Every set it empties is one the rulebot posterior already gives zero mass -- reachable only through
the `Pass` line, which no play-first policy takes. So it never contradicts the opponent it assumes,
and **every score in sections 2 and 7.3 stands**: Random and the Heuristic both satisfy it.

### 8.4 A second finding: the uniform split is not the exact conditional -- **on forced play only**

Independent of the constraint, `determinize_l1`'s uniform split is wrong once the history contains
draws **on a forced-play ruleset**, because hands consistent with more pile orders carry more
worlds. The scope matters and is measured in 8.7: on **free play**, which `FULL_UNO` is, the
uniform split is *exactly* the conditional, so nothing in this subsection reaches the full-UNO
numbers. It is a property of the toys. A `TOY` set with the history `setup, play, draw`:

| opponent hand | exact | uniform split | member worlds |
|---|---|---|---|
| (0, 2, 3, 4) | 0.1250 | 0.1965 | 1 |
| (0, 2, 3, 5) | 0.1250 | 0.2023 | 1 |
| (0, 2, 4, 5) | 0.1250 | 0.1990 | 1 |
| (0, 3, 4, 5) | 0.1250 | 0.2024 | 1 |
| **(2, 3, 4, 5)** | **0.5000** | 0.1998 | 4 |

Opening sets match exactly (0.1000 against 0.0955-0.1037 at 20,000 draws), so on a forced-play
ruleset the module's claim that the uniform split "is the conditional the deal model implies once
the public history is fixed" holds only while nobody has drawn -- and on a free-play one it holds
outright (8.7). The useful framing, again toy-side: **`voids` is a hard 0/1 approximation of a soft
reweighting the tree computes exactly.**

### 8.5 Why nothing caught it, and what now does

Every existing test inspects rulebot games, which never take the `Pass` line, so
`test_voids_never_exclude_the_hand_the_opponent_really_holds` verified a scoped fact while reading
as a universal one -- a test that passes with the defect present, the step 9.5e lesson one level
over. `tests/eval/test_belief.py` now runs **both** opponents in one test and demands 0 violations
from the rulebot and more than 0 from a passing opponent, so the assumption fails loudly if it is
ever quietly relied on again.

### 8.6 Decision, and what it opens

`since = 0` is **kept** and the assumption stated, since the alternative is a rung that does
nothing. The wording is corrected in `uno/eval/belief.py`, spec section 26.2, the context doc and
`index.md`.

The interesting consequence is that this makes `voids` a **deliberately exploitable opponent** with
a known signature, which is worth more than a caveat: a best response to a `voids` row should
discover that keeping a playable drawn card walks into worlds the row has ruled out. That turns the
unmeasured exploitability of item 3 from a box to tick into a prediction with a mechanism, and it
gives steps 10-11 a test subject whose exploit is known in advance. The warning for step 11 is the
same finding read forwards: an RL agent that learns to hold a playable drawn card breaks the
assumption, so `voids` is not automatically safe as a general-player component.

### 8.8 `soft`: believing the void instead of enforcing it

Since 8.2 shows the assumption cannot be removed and 8.7 shows no sound alternative exists on free
play, the remaining move is to stop treating it as certain. `"soft"` weights a hand by
`decay ** max(0, playable - since)` per void rather than rejecting it, so `decay = 0` reproduces
`voids` exactly, `decay = 1` the uniform prior, and anything between is a one-parameter family. All
three properties are pinned by tests.

| opponent | `voids` puts zero on the truth | `soft` does | soft truth weight | discrimination |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rulebot | 0.0 % | **0.0 %** | 1.000 | 2.71 |
| passer | 12.3 % | **0.0 %** | 0.908 | 2.33 |
| bluffer | 68.5 % | **0.0 %** | 0.359 | 1.14 |

It does the one thing it was built to do -- it never excludes the truth -- and against a bluffer it
decays towards the prior instead of excluding reality, which is "trust their draws less" arising
from the decay itself rather than from detection. Detection could not have carried it: the
public-only test `played_playable > since + 1` has perfect precision (0 false positives in 150
honest games) but only 7-14 % recall, saturating by p = 0.1, because a bluffer who draws more also
raises the very threshold that would convict them.

**The decay matters, and the first guess was the worst setting.** `decay = 0.25` -- taken from the
odds a drawn card is playable -- is the only value measured to cost score. Paired against `voids`
over 2,400 DEV deals against the honest column:

| decay | seat 0 | seat 1 |
|---|---|---|
| 0.02 | -0.71 (z -0.7) | -0.29 (z -0.3) |
| 0.05 | -0.38 (z -0.4) | +0.75 (z +0.7) |
| **0.10** | +0.42 (z +0.4) | +1.54 (z +1.4) |
| 0.25 | **-2.25 (z -2.2)** | -0.12 (z -0.1) |

At `decay <= 0.1` nothing is separable from `voids` at either seat, so the default is **0.1**. Not
because it scored best: +0.42 / +1.54 is not significant, and taking the argmax of four DEV configs
is the winner's curse this step keeps meeting. The tie-break is robustness -- among settings
indistinguishable on score, the softest.

**Marginal accuracy and joint accuracy disagree, and 9.5f already said which to trust.** At
`decay = 0.1`, `soft` is the best-calibrated rung there is -- Brier **0.0826** against `voids`'
0.0828 and the prior's 0.0846, worst reliability-bin gap **0.080** against 0.093 / 0.135 / 0.088,
and **0 % fallbacks** against `voids`' 4.8 % -- yet it is the *worse* belief: TV to the exact
posterior **0.1838 / 0.1528** against `voids`' **0.0502 / 0.0407**, closing 58-66 % of the distance
where `voids` closes 88-91 %. That is section 4's lesson reappearing one level over: the constraint
rules out *joint* combinations while barely moving any single card's marginal, so Brier is the
right correctness test and the wrong value test. `soft` never over-commits, and also never
sharpens; `voids` does both.

**And the exploit does not pay.** The row scores **0.98-0.99 against the bluffer**: voluntary
drawing costs cards and tempo far more than the deception gains, so wrecking the belief does not
win the game. A *selective* exploiter -- bluffing only when it profits, which is what an RL agent
would learn -- is the untested case.

So: **`voids` remains the default for the ladder**, whose columns are honest and where its
assumption therefore holds exactly, and it is the sharper belief. **`soft` at `decay = 0.1` is the
rung for an opponent that might not be honest** -- it never excludes the truth, never falls back,
is better calibrated, and costs nothing this measurement can resolve. Whether its worse joint
accuracy costs score below that resolution is genuinely open: the sweep bounds it at about one
point, not at zero.

### 8.7 Is there a better pruner? Sound pruning is nearly exhausted; weighting is not

The obvious follow-up -- if `voids` prunes by assumption, is there a *sound* pruner that does
better? -- is measured, over a uniform sample of 2,001 constrained `TOY` sets per seat:

| | seat 0 | seat 1 |
|---|---|---|
| constrained sets where **every** rules-possible hand occurs (nothing to prune soundly) | 94.9 % | 84.0 % |
| sets containing any hand impossible in every consistent world | 5.0 % | 15.9 % |
| median share of rules-possible hands that do occur | **1.00** | **1.00** |
| share of *genuinely consistent* hands `voids` keeps | **0.74** | **0.76** |

Two readings, and they point the same way:

1. **A sound pruner would prune less than `voids`, not more.** In the typical constrained set there
   is nothing to exclude on the rules alone (median share 1.00). Meanwhile `voids` discards about a
   quarter of the hands that really are possible. Its force comes from being right about the
   *opponent*, not about the rules -- section 8.2 from a different angle.
2. **The sound information is a weight, not a filter.** At hand level the question is "does there
   *exist* a drawn card making the pre-draw hand legal", which is almost always yes -- that is why
   the sound constraint is a tautology (8.2). At world level it is "does the *specific* card the
   pile order says was drawn make it legal", which often fails. So what carries information is how
   **many** pile orders realise a hand: the multiplicity of 8.4, worth TV 0.43 / 0.45.

**The option that is actually better is therefore not a pruner.** Sample *worlds* rather than
hands: draw a (hand, pile order) pair and keep the ones consistent with replaying the opponent's
observed **draw events**, which `determinize_l1` never conditions on (it conditions on hand sizes,
the discard and the setup tail). That is sound -- no policy assumption, so it survives into a
general player -- and it captures 8.4 automatically, since hands realised by more orders are
proposed more often. It is this module's own stated lesson, "condition, never weight", applied one
level deeper than the rungs apply it. Build it by **construction rather than rejection** (choose
which of the current hand were drawn and replace them on the pile in order): acceptance for 7 cards
out of ~98 unseen is likely poor on full UNO. Penalty draws fall out of the same logic for free.

The instrument already exists: a sound sampler should close against the **policy-free prior**,
which is exactly the target the first, mis-specified version of this test used (8.1). That control
becomes the right test the moment the sampler stops assuming an opponent.

**What it would buy is correctness, not strength.** It prunes less than `voids`, so against the
rulebots -- which satisfy `voids`' assumption exactly (8.3) -- the score should fall slightly; it
should hold up where `voids` breaks. Against known rulebot columns `voids` is already 89-91 % of
the way to the posterior and there is little left to win.

**And it does not transfer to full UNO at all -- measured, and this withdraws the proposal.** The
check has teeth only where the rules constrain *drawing*. On **forced play** `legal_actions` offers
`Draw` only when nothing is playable, so the pile order fixes which card was drawn and its removal
must leave a legal pre-draw hand -- that is what produces 8.4's 4-against-1 multiplicities. On
**free play** drawing is always legal, the rules say nothing about why it happened, and card
conservation and the public hand sizes are already conditioned on.

Measured on a matched 7-card pair (3 colours x 2 ranks + 1 wild, hand 2, `reshuffle="none"`,
identical but for `forced_play`), over every information set following an opponent draw, on full
non-symmetric world trees so multiplicities survive:

| | sets after a draw | some rules-possible hand absent | present hands unequally weighted | mean TV to uniform |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| forced play, seat 0 | 5,580 | 6.1 % | 0.9 % | 0.0022 |
| forced play, seat 1 | 5,928 | 22.9 % | 3.0 % | 0.0051 |
| **free play, seat 0** | 54,696 | **0.0 %** | **0.0 %** | **0.0000** |
| **free play, seat 1** | 38,958 | **0.0 %** | **0.0 %** | **0.0000** |

Exact zeros over ~94,000 free-play sets: structurally nothing, which is what the argument predicts
rather than a small measured effect. Three consequences:

1. **The sound sampler above is withdrawn**, not merely deprioritised. `FULL_UNO` is free play, so
   it would be a no-op precisely where it is wanted.
2. **`voids`' entire measured full-UNO value rests on the policy assumption of 8.2**, and **no
   sound replacement for it exists on a free-play ruleset**. It is not a correctable approximation:
   on free play, a policy assumption is the *only* kind of inference available about the
   opponent's hand.
3. **8.4 is a toy artefact.** `determinize_l1`'s uniform split is exactly right on full UNO, so the
   belief numbers of sections 2 and 7.3 are untouched by it.

For step 11 that reframes the warning of 8.6: the problem is not that `voids` uses an assumption
where a sound alternative was available, but that on the ruleset that matters there is no
alternative at all -- so an opponent that stops satisfying the assumption cannot be answered by
better inference, only by a different one.
