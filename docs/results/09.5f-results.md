## What was built

- `uno/eval/belief.py`: the belief over the opponent's hand, as three rungs -- `prior` (the
  uniform split, i.e. the 9.5e row unchanged), `voids` (the hard constraints a draw implies) and
  `window` (plus the opponent's real action probabilities over their trailing run of plays).
- `uno/eval/search.py`: `determinize_l1(..., belief=...)` and `PimcSearch(belief=...)`. Without a
  belief the behaviour is byte-identical to 9.5e's, which the 22 pre-existing tests still pin.
- `uno/experiments/pimc.py`: a `belief` section (`uv run uno pimc belief`).
- Tests: `tests/eval/test_belief.py`, 9 tests in 0.70 s.

## 1. The design in the plan does not work

`plan.md` specified a **particle filter over the deal**: sample the opponent's initial hand,
replay the public history, weight each particle by the opponent's action probabilities, resample
per step. Built first, and measured before any score was believed:

| opponent's play | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| share of 64 particles still alive | 0.156 | 0.018 | 0.002 | 0.0004 | 0 |

A particle survives an observed play only if it happens to hold the played card, which is
`hand_size / unseen` ~ 7/98 on full UNO. The decay is geometric, so **no particle count fixes it**:
by the fourth play one would need ~10^5 particles, and it keeps decaying. The implementation that
reset on collapse (62 % of decisions) scored **worse than the prior it replaced** -- Brier 0.167
against the uniform split's 0.085, with its "probability 1.000" bin observed at 0.112.

This was caught by a calibration harness written *before* the score was measured, not by the score
itself. Sharpness alone would have looked like progress; calibration is what says a belief is
*correct* rather than merely confident. The lesson generalises: **condition, never weight.** A
world must be built consistent with what was observed, not sampled and then tested against it.

## 2. The belief ladder (`uv run uno pimc belief 3000 32 2`)

3,000 EVAL deals per rung, both seatings, w = 32, d = 2, response continuation, against Random.
Paired on the same deals.

| belief | seat 0 | seat 1 | ms/game |
|---|---|---|---|
| prior (the 9.5e row) | 0.7530 +- 0.0079 | 0.7163 +- 0.0082 | 126.5 |
| **voids** | **0.7813 +- 0.0075** | **0.7603 +- 0.0078** | 139.6 |
| **window** | **0.7907 +- 0.0074** | 0.7517 +- 0.0079 | 162.9 |

| comparison | seat 0 | seat 1 |
|---|---|---|
| voids - prior | **+2.83 +- 0.95 (z +3.0)** | **+4.40 +- 0.99 (z +4.4)** |
| window - prior | **+3.77 +- 0.94 (z +4.0)** | **+3.53 +- 1.02 (z +3.5)** |
| window - voids | +0.93 +- 0.90 (z +1.0) | -0.87 +- 0.95 (z -0.9) |

Readings:

1. **Inference is established at both seats**, for both rungs, at 3 sigma or better. It is the
   second-largest full-UNO lever found, after 9.5e's continuation rule (+9.73 / +4.57).
2. **The soft likelihood buys nothing measurable over the hard constraints.** `window - voids` is
   +0.93 / -0.87 -- opposite signs at the two seats and both inside one sigma. The whole gain is
   the rung that reads **no opponent policy at all**.
3. **So the cheap half is the half worth keeping.** `voids` costs +10 % (139.6 against 126.5
   ms/game) where `window` costs +29 %, and -- unlike the `response` continuation and unlike
   `window` -- it does not make the row opponent-specific, so it is the part that survives into a
   general player. `voids` is the recommended default.
4. **The pre-declared prediction is confirmed.** The toys measure `posterior - prior` at 0.0-0.6
   points against Random (up to 2.6 against the Heuristic), which is why 9.5e deferred this step.
   Full UNO gives **+2.83 / +4.40**, five to ten times the toy figure against the same column. The
   mechanism predicted when the step was scoped is what `voids` exploits: a full-UNO game runs 41
   decisions with `Draw` legal at 60 % of them, so the opponent's draws are frequent and each one
   is a hard statement, where a 6-decision toy has almost none. This is 9.5d's "toy belief sizes
   do not transfer" pointing the other way.
5. **Do not read the per-seat ordering.** On DEV the gain was larger at seat 0 (+4.20 / +1.80); on
   EVAL it is larger at seat 1 for `voids`. Individual seats move by more than their difference;
   only the both-seats claim is supported.

## 3. Why the constraint is worth so much

Measured on full UNO before the rung was written: the void constraint alone rules out **79.9 %** of
`determinize_l1`'s worlds (median surviving share 15.6 %, so **w = 64 is effectively w = 10**), and
the opponent's real hand violated it **0 times in 2,487** decisions. At least one constraint is
live at **73.4 %** of full-UNO decisions.

That also bears on 9.5e's open question. That step found the world ladder still flat past 32 with
the continuation and the seat networks both fixed, and promoted the off-profile leaf network to
"the residual explanation". A second explanation was available and is now measured: **most of the
worlds were impossible**, so the extra ones were not adding information. Whether the ladder
un-flattens once the belief is correct is the natural next test and is **not** settled here.

## 4. Checks

- **Calibration, not sharpness**, is the correctness test, and it is what refuted section 1. On
  120 DEV deals over 120,712 (card type, decision) pairs, Brier: prior 0.0846, `voids` 0.0828,
  `window` 0.0825, with predicted tracking observed in every populated bin (0.041 -> 0.050,
  0.142 -> 0.135, 0.234 -> 0.208, 0.428 -> 0.396). A belief that over-prunes is sharper *and*
  mis-calibrated; this one is neither.
- **The marginal sharpening is small (2 %) while 79.9 % of worlds are excluded**, which is not a
  contradiction: the constraint rules out joint combinations while barely moving any single card's
  marginal. Brier on marginals is the right *correctness* check and the wrong *value* check, which
  is why section 2 is scored and not inferred from it.
- `tests/eval/test_belief.py` pins the two truth-survives properties (`voids` never excludes the
  hand the opponent really holds, on `FULL_UNO` and `TOY_FULL`; the `window` likelihood is
  strictly positive for it), a non-vacuity guard, world consistency, and that the agent is still a
  deterministic function of its information state with a belief wired in.
- **The `belief` run's "0 failed checks" line means nothing.** That section registers no `check()`
  assertions of its own, unlike `uno` and `d2`, so its counter is trivially zero. Validation for
  this step is the tests plus `uv run uno pimc calibration`, which *does* carry real checks (no
  rung materially worse than the exact prior; no populated reliability bin missing by more than
  0.25, where the refuted filter missed by 0.89) and reports the fallback rates that `belief`
  cannot, since it runs single-process. `belief` itself still needs checks.

## 6. What is left on the table

1. **`window` is untested where it should matter.** The column here is Random, which is nearly
   uniform, so its play choices carry little information -- and the toys agree, putting
   `posterior - prior` at 0.0-0.6 against Random but up to **2.6 against the Heuristic**. The
   vs-Heuristic column is where the soft rung should earn its 29 %, and it has not been run.
2. **No toy closure test for this axis.** Every other sampled method in 9.5b-9.5e was closed
   against an exact toy value, and this one is not: `window` is a *windowed* posterior, so it need
   not equal `uno.compiled.pimc`'s `"posterior"`, and what it should equal has not been derived.
   `TOY2` vs the Heuristic at seat 1 separates the belief axis by 2.62 points and `voids` is live
   there (23.3 % of decisions), so the cell exists; the assertion does not yet.
3. **Exploitability is unmeasured and the toys warn about it.** There, fitting the belief to one
   opponent *costs* exploitability (`TOY2_SKIP` vs the Heuristic: 3.52 / 6.43 for
   `posterior/response` against 1.78 / 2.29 for `prior/response`). `voids` should be exempt, since
   it reads no policy, but that is an argument and not a measurement.
4. **Does the world ladder un-flatten?** Section 3: the belief roughly sextuples the effective
   world count, so the w = 32 -> 128 ladder should be re-run with `voids` on. If it now climbs,
   the leaf network is *not* the binding constraint 9.5e concluded it was.
5. **The `belief` section still registers no checks** (section 4) and its fallback-rate line is
   inert under multiprocessing, the counters not crossing `pmap`'s process boundary. The
   calibration harness was promoted into the repo as `uv run uno pimc calibration` with two real
   checks and working fallback rates (4.8 % / 4.9 %), but `belief` itself was left as it is.
6. **`window` is the least calibrated rung** -- worst reliability-bin gap 0.135 against `voids`'
   0.093 and the prior's 0.088 -- while being marginally the sharpest (Brier 0.0825). That is what
   a *windowed* posterior should look like, conditioning on a truncated history and so slightly
   biased, and it is a second mark against `window` beside its failure to beat `voids` on score.
